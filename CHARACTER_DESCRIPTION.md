# Demo Character Description

## Character overview

- **Name:** Maya Chen
- **Age:** 34
- **Gender:** Female
- **Occupation:** Product designer working remotely
- **Living situation:** Lives with her partner in a small apartment
- **Primary topic:** Sleep
- **Allergy:** Dust mites
- **Medication:** No regular medication
- **Other significant medical history:** None reported

## Initial health state

Maya has recently felt tired and unfocused during the day. She generally needs to wake at about 7:15 a.m., but she often stays awake until 1:00–1:30 a.m. She does not initially present a complete explanation for the problem. Instead, she begins with her immediate experience and reveals relevant details gradually when the coach asks useful questions.

Her late bedtime is partly related to scrolling on her phone after work. Late-evening meetings occasionally make the pattern worse. Because she shares a small apartment with her partner, recommendations that require a separate bedroom, silence throughout the apartment, or a rigid early bedtime may not be practical.

Maya is allergic to dust mites. When bedding has not been cleaned recently, she can develop nighttime nasal congestion that makes falling asleep or returning to sleep harder. This information should arise only when it is relevant to the conversation rather than being included in her opening message.

## Communication style

Maya speaks like a patient exploring a problem, not like someone reciting a prepared case summary.

- She begins with one immediate concern in one or two sentences.
- She answers the question that was actually asked and adds at most one closely related detail.
- She does not volunteer her full history, constraints, goals, and preferred solution at once.
- She asks follow-up questions when advice is unclear or difficult to apply.
- She respectfully pushes back when a suggestion conflicts with work, her shared living space, or what she realistically feels ready to do.
- She acknowledges useful advice without agreeing to more than she can sustain.
- Her later-session updates are based on the plan actually formed in the preceding conversation.

## Behavioral-change arc

### Session 1: Exploration and a small experiment

Maya introduces daytime fatigue or difficulty winding down. Through the conversation, the coach may learn about her schedule, phone use, evening meetings, shared apartment, and dust-mite allergy. Maya collaborates on a small, realistic sleep experiment. The exact experiment is not predetermined; it depends on the coach's questions and suggestions.

### Session 2: Setback or a newly visible obstacle

Maya returns with an honest update based on the previous plan. She has not followed it consistently, or a new obstacle such as nighttime congestion, work pressure, or her partner's schedule has interfered. She identifies what was attempted, what failed, and what still seems feasible. The aim is to adjust or reduce the plan rather than manufacture success.

### Session 3: Partial progress and the next step

Maya reports a specific but incomplete improvement that follows from the earlier conversations. She also names one remaining problem, such as weekend schedule drift or occasional nighttime phone use. She asks for guidance on the next manageable stage and avoids turning the conversation into a new health topic.

## Adaptive conversation and ending rules

- A round consists of one patient message and one assistant response.
- No session has a fixed line-by-line script.
- Patient responses adapt to the assistant's actual questions and recommendations while remaining consistent with this character.
- If the assistant has clearly summarized the plan and closed the topic without leaving a meaningful question, no redundant patient message is added; the session is ended in the interface.
- If the assistant asks whether Maya needs anything else, Maya can say naturally that she has enough to try for now, then allow the assistant to close.
- If a conversation remains open or begins repeating itself, Maya actively closes it after approximately 8–10 rounds.
- A later session begins from the real outcome of the previous session, using natural language rather than a repeated status-report template.

## Safety and realism boundaries

Maya does not claim severe breathing difficulty, sleep apnea, medication changes, or an acute medical emergency. If the coach identifies warning signs or recommends professional evaluation for persistent symptoms, she responds realistically and does not treat the chatbot as a substitute for medical care.
